## Problem

RoboSub asks a student-built AUV to complete an underwater course **fully autonomously**: pass a gate, slalom through
a channel, drop markers into a bin and fire torpedoes through small openings. There is no GPS underwater,
visibility is poor and pool time is scarce. The vehicle has to hold depth and heading against buoyancy and currents
on six thrusters, and the software has to be testable when the sub is **not** in the water. The team logged 70 h of
simulation testing for the 2026 entry.

## Approach

### The control pipeline

  steps={[

  ]}
  feedback="BehaviorTree mission planner: one subtree per task, actions re-tried when a condition is lost"
/>

- **Thrust allocation.** The allocator builds the 6×6 map from each thruster's force direction and lever arm about the
  centre of mass, two of them angled, and inverts it with an SVD pseudo-inverse. Each desired force and torque becomes
  per-thruster thrust, which is encoded into 5-bit levels within the T200's forward and reverse limits (3.71 / 2.92 kgf)
  and streamed over USB serial to a Teensy that drives the ESCs.
- **Control.** I designed and tuned the **PID controller** that runs on every degree of freedom, with gains exposed
  as ROS 2 parameters so they can be retuned on the pool deck. The tuning reduced steady-state error by **32 %**.
- **Leading the team.** Nine engineers worked in parallel through documented package conventions, an issue/PR
  workflow and Docker images that deploy the whole stack to a spare single-board computer in minutes.

### A digital twin before pool time

I brought the vehicle into **Gazebo**: the URDF/SDF model with the real frame meshes, inertia and damping, plus the
team's plugins for buoyancy and hydrodynamic forces, thruster drivers, pressure and camera sensors, and the
competition props modelled to scale. Controllers and missions could be developed and replayed without the pool, which
**halved development time**.

### Perception

Gates and markers are found by HSV segmentation and convex hulls after lens and enclosure undistortion, giving
relative poses as control targets. The 2026 stack adds a YOLOv8 detector fine-tuned on Unity-generated underwater
images, and an Intel RealSense D455 with RTAB-Map for a redundant map of the course.

## Results

- The team placed in the **top 10 of about 200 teams** at RoboSub.
- PID control: **−32 % steady-state error**. Gazebo twin: **−50 % development time**. Both are from the team's own testing.
- Steelhead's design, from machined aluminium enclosures and custom neoprene seals to the software and acoustic
  localization, is documented in the public
  [technical design report](https://robonation.org/app/uploads/sites/4/2026/07/RS26_TDR_UnivBritishColumbia.pdf), which
  I co-authored, and in the [Steelhead repository](https://github.com/ubc-subbots/steelhead).

## What I learned

- **Leading engineers is mostly about interfaces.** Clear topic contracts between estimation, waypoints, control and
  allocation let nine people work in parallel without breaking each other's code.
- **Simulation pays for itself fast** when real test time is the bottleneck. The digital twin was the highest-leverage
  thing we built.
- **Keep the last mile simple.** A pseudo-inverse allocator and a serial link to a microcontroller are easy to reason
  about during a pool test; the sophistication belongs in the layers above.
