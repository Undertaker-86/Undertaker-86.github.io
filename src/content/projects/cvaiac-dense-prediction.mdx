## Problem

An autonomous car needs to know **what** each pixel is and **how far away** it is. The task was one multi-task model
for 19-class semantic segmentation and monocular depth. It was trained on 25 000 synthetic driving images and
ranked on a hidden 2 500-image test set by a single multitask score (segmentation mIoU minus depth SI-logRMSE).

## Approach

The project ran in two stages: first build and understand a strong model from its parts, then spend a fixed 48-hour
GPU budget on the best model we could deploy.

### Stage 1: DeepLabV3+ from the building blocks

- ASPP, task-specific decoders and skip connections were implemented by hand in PyTorch and ablated one at a time.
- An Optuna campaign (TPE sampler, pruning) searched architecture and training choices together, and multi-scale
  inference was added on top: validation score **69.84 → 76.68**, test **76.69**.
- PyTorch Lightning and W&B recorded every run's configuration, checkpoint, metrics and hardware use: 87 runs.

<Figure src={arch} alt="Architecture diagram of the multi-task DeepLabV3+ network" caption="The stage-1 multi-task architecture." />

### Stage 2: a foundation model, adapted to two tasks

- **Backbone:** the official, pinned **Depth Anything 3 Metric Large** checkpoint: a ViT-L/14 encoder with a DPT depth
  head.
- **Second head:** a copy of the DPT head becomes a 19-class segmentation decoder that shares the encoder (features
  from layers 4, 11, 17, 23). It inherits the pretrained reconstruction weights; only its final classifier is new.
- **Training:** cross-entropy + Lovász for segmentation, SILog for depth, a learnable depth scale initialised from
  training labels only, and separate AdamW learning rates for encoder, depth head and segmentation head. BF16 with
  activation checkpointing fits it on one RTX 5080.
- **Budget-aware tuning:** a small Optuna search with 12-epoch trials and pruning, then a fresh 24-epoch retrain of the
  winner. Everything was accounted against the 48 GPU-hour budget (41.5 h used).
- **Test-time augmentation:** scales 0.75 / 1 / 1.25 with horizontal flips and geometric averaging of depth. This beat
  an ensemble with the stage-1 ResNet model (80.35).

## Results

The final submission placed **1st** on the course leaderboard: **81.15** multitask, **91.65** mIoU and **10.5**
SI-logRMSE, ahead of 80.28 for 2nd place and 74.36 for 3rd.

## What I learned

- **Know the model, then pick the best one.** Building DeepLabV3+ by hand made it clear what a foundation model brings:
  depth features that a 25 000-image dataset can't teach.
- **Treat compute as a budget.** Short pruned trials, then one long retrain of the winner, got the most out of 48 GPU
  hours.
- **Trust validation you've audited.** Exact export round-trips on the full validation split made the leaderboard
  result predictable (81.11 val vs 81.15 test).
