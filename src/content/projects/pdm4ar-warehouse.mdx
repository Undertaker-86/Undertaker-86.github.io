## Problem

One to eight differential-drive robots share a warehouse with obstacles. They must pick up goals and deliver each one
to any collection point within 60 s. A robot carries one goal at a time, picks up any goal it drives into, and delivers
whenever it crosses a collection point while loaded. At run time each robot sees only a limited, occluded 2D LiDAR scan,
and the robots **cannot communicate**. The score rewards finishing early and penalises everything else:

$$
\text{score} = 100\,G_{\text{delivered}} - 500\,N_{\text{collided}} + 10\,(60 - T_{\text{last}}) - 0.5\,D - 0.01\,E - \text{penalties}
$$

Reading the score as a specification set the design. One second of makespan is worth 10 points, while one second of
driving costs about 1.2 points per robot, so **robots should always drive at full speed and the work must be balanced**.
With the kinematic limit $|v| + 0.5|\omega| \le 2$, a path takes exactly $(L + 0.5\,\Theta)/2$ seconds, where $\Theta$ is
the total turning. Driving backwards is free, so a reversal at a goal costs nothing.

## Approach

The simulator is deterministic and every robot knows its own state exactly. A central planner can therefore compute
the **complete timed trajectory of every robot**, check it, and send it once. Each robot then just executes its
plan, with no need to react to the others.

  feedback="the plan string is the only communication; each robot tracks it with an arc-fitting controller (≈ 1 ms per step)"

- **Geometry.** The free space is the map shrunk by the robot radius plus a margin, with obstacles inflated by
  conservative polygons. Shortest paths come from a visibility graph. Pickups use via points just inside each goal and
  collection disk, so paths only **graze** them instead of driving to their centres, saving up to 1.8 m per delivery.
  Every leg also avoids other goals, so a robot never picks something up by accident.
- **Allocation.** An exact backward DP over goal subsets gives the cheapest way for each robot to serve any set of
  goals. Splits between robots are searched exactly (up to 12 goals) or by local search, minimising
  makespan plus distance in the same proportions as the score.
- **Coordination.** Each robot's timing comes from an advance-or-wait DP over (path sample, time) against the robots
  already planned. When a leg would need long waits, the planner tries other via points, other collection points and
  detours, and takes the earliest arrival. A search over priority orders and repair of the latest robot (LNS) follow.
  Finished robots park somewhere safe forever.
- **Verification.** Before anything is sent, the whole plan is replayed at the simulator's 0.01 s resolution with
  the exact pickup, delivery and 60 s rules. Static clearance of at least the robot radius + 2.5 cm and robot-robot
  clearance of at least 1.23 m are enforced. **Collisions and deadlocks are ruled out by construction.**
- **Over-full maps.** When not every goal can be delivered in 60 s, the objective switches to orienteering: deliver
  as many goals as possible before the cut-off.

## What I learned

- **Turn the grader into design rules.** Full speed always, balance the makespan, and backwards driving is free. Those
  three facts decided most of the architecture before I wrote any code.
- **When a system is deterministic, verify instead of reacting.** Exact replay turned a hard online multi-robot problem
  into an offline search with a correctness check, which is why there are no collisions.
- **Know how far from optimal you are.** Deriving upper bounds showed that config 2 was already at 95 % of what any
  planner could reach, so effort went to the instances with real headroom.
- **Measure every change.** Each improvement was A/B-tested on 45–60 configs. Incremental scheduling (+1 931 points in
  total) and LNS repair with a 4× faster DP (+3 519) mattered most; more candidates or via points added nothing and
  were dropped.
