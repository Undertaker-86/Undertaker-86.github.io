## Problem

Headers are the most frequent head impact in soccer, and repeated heading has been linked to cognitive decline and
structural changes in the brain. Measuring exposure today means wearable sensors, which are inaccurate, or people
watching footage, which does not scale (one study needed 135 matches of manual review to log ~9 000 headers). The
question was whether headers can be found **automatically and accurately in ordinary broadcast video**.

A header lasts a few frames, the ball is a few pixels wide in a wide broadcast shot, and almost every frame is
negative. That makes the problem a needle-in-a-haystack action-recognition problem.

## Approach

  label="Header detection pipeline from broadcast video to header events"
  steps={[

    { title: 'RF-DETR ball detector', sub: 'SoccerNet-fine-tuned; top-k ball candidates per frame' },
    { title: 'Kalman smoothing', sub: 'constant-velocity [x, y, vx, vy] removes misses and distractors' },
    { title: 'Ball-centred 16-frame clips', sub: 'crop follows the smoothed ball', kind: 'model' },
    { title: 'VideoMAE V2 classifier', sub: 'Base (87 M) / Giant (1 B); focal loss, 10:1 neg:pos sampling', kind: 'model' },
  ]}
/>

- **Look where the ball is.** Cropping each clip around the Kalman-smoothed ball trajectory beat feeding the full
  frame. The model sees the head–ball contact at a usable resolution.
- **Dense, reproducible preprocessing.** Every frame becomes one row of a Parquet dataset with its smoothed ball box
  and label. Positives are widened to a window matched to the video's frame rate, and clips that would run off the
  video are dropped, so training needs no runtime padding.
- **Imbalance handled twice.** Focal loss down-weights easy negatives, and a deterministic 10:1 negative:positive
  sampler keeps every epoch comparable.
- **Scale and tune.** The Giant backbone runs with DDP, mixed precision and gradient checkpointing on 24 GB GPUs
  under Slurm. Optuna then searches focal α, γ and the sampling ratio per backbone.

Optuna tuning then added about **5 points of recall and 3 of precision**. The final model reaches **70.4 % recall at
80.3 % precision** on held-out test matches, close to the 80 %/80 % target set at the start.

## What I learned

- **Put the inductive bias in the data.** One preprocessing decision, cropping around a tracked ball, mattered more
  than any architecture change.
- **Infrastructure is a research result.** Moving to Slurm with DDP and AMP halved training time. That is what made
  a Giant backbone and an Optuna search affordable at all.
- **Report operating points, not one number.** For a screening tool the useful question is "how many headers do we
  miss at a precision researchers can work with?", so I report full PR curves and fixed-precision/recall points.
