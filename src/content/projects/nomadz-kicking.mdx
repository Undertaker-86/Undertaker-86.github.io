## Problem

In RoboCup humanoid soccer a kick that merely *touches* the ball is useless. The robot has to walk up to a ball that
may still be rolling, plant its support foot, and put the ball **inside a 2.6 m goal** from up to 4.5 m away. It does
this from its own noisy, delayed camera estimate of the ball, and it must not fall. The K1's firmware ships a scripted
kick, but it is open-loop: it neither aims nor adapts to where the ball actually is.

The goal was one learned skill that does approach, kick and aim together and survives the jump to a different
physics engine, as a first step towards the real robot.

## Approach

### Part 1: eight ways to learn a kick, tested on the same shots

Rather than bet on one method, I set up a fair comparison. Every approach ("arm") was evaluated on identical,
seeded scenarios in **MuJoCo**, a different physics engine from the Isaac Lab training simulator. That makes every
result a sim-to-sim transfer test.

| Arm | Approach | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| A0 | Firmware kick (scripted baseline) | open loop in sim: falls every time |
| **A1** | **PPO + adversarial motion prior (AMP) on firmware kick clips + task reward** | **best-transferring learned kick** |
| A2 | Tracker of the firmware kick + kick inputs, two stages | stage 2 did not converge |
| A3 | Pure PPO, no motion prior | swings at the ball; worst transfer |
| A4 / A5 | Multi-clip motion trackers (incl. a SuperMimic port to the K1) | track held-out clips, strike too imprecise |
| A6 | Diffusion policy trained on A1's rollouts | matches A1 with fewer falls, ~16× slower to run |
| A7a / A7b | Firmware tracker / residual RL on top of it | A7b is the most improved arm |

The first benchmark asked for a kick in a *commanded direction*. Learned arms touched the ball 93–95 % of the time,
but the median direction error was 23–28°, flat across directions: scatter, not bias. So I moved to the task that
actually matters, **scoring in a goal**, with a 5 000-shot benchmark (1.5–4.5 m, up to ±50° off-axis).

1. **Goal reward v2:** a distance factor, an on-target launch-speed reward, revoking credit after a re-touch, a
   fall claw-back, 15 s episodes, and training with both torque modes.
2. **Longer credit assignment:** the set-up step happens ~0.5 s before the kick is paid, so γ 0.997, λ 0.99 and
   64-step rollouts.
3. **The ball-rolling fix, the biggest surprise.** Training randomised rolling resistance from 0.4 to 1.2, while the
   field fit is 0.64. At the high end, far shots were *physically unreachable*, so the policy learned to kick harder
   instead of straighter. Narrowing the range to 0.45–0.85 unblocked aim.
4. **Set-up factor:** a first touch before 0.3 s pays 0.3×, which cut rushed first touches from 15 % to 2 % of far shots.
5. **Longer ball history:** 300 ms of past detections instead of 80 ms. It only helped *after* the rolling fix let
   aim improve.

Eight further ideas did not beat the best: lower KL, a reach reward, oversampling, weight averaging, a hand-built
ball filter as input, lower action noise and more. Training past a peak always drifted down, so the process became
"restart from the best, change one thing".

Beyond perception, the policy is strongly **right-footed**: left-foot kicks are 13 % of far shots and score 39 %, against
80 % with the right foot. Balls very close to the robot, balls off to the side and steep approach angles cost 9–17
points each, and mesh-shaped foot colliders score a few points below box feet, which matters for the real robot.

## What I learned

- **Compare approaches on the same shots, in a different simulator.** Pure PPO had the *best* clean-goal rate in Isaac
  Lab and the worst in MuJoCo. Only a cross-engine benchmark exposed that.
- **Measure the noise floor before tuning.** Quick screens moved ±3 points. Deciding only on full validations
  (±1 point) stopped me from "improving" the policy by chance.
- **Fix the physics before the reward.** An over-wide randomisation range made the task impossible in a third of the
  environments, and no reward change could fix that.
- **Separate the error budget.** Swapping the camera model showed that ~17 far points are lost in perception. That
  makes a learned ball estimator the next step, ahead of more kick training.
- The [detailed write-up](/projects/nomadz-kicking/details) covers the policy interface, domain randomisation,
  reward terms, the benchmark protocol and the full training changelog.
- Also in this role: a video → motion pipeline (GVHMR → SMPL, GMR retargeting to the K1, DeepMimic imitation in
  Isaac Lab, MuJoCo sim-to-sim via Booster Deploy) and RL policies for passing.
