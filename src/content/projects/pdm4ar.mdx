## Problem

**Docking.** A single-thruster spaceship with state $[x, y, \psi, v_x, v_y, \omega, \delta, m]$ must fly past planets
and *moving* asteroids, turn around and reverse into a docking bay. The bay's arms leave only a narrow gap. An
unknown external push may hit it on the way. The grader rewards short, cheap, accurate flights and bills computation
inside the control loop:

$$
\text{score} = 1000 - 10\left(\tfrac{d}{5} + \tfrac{t}{3} + \overline{|F|} + \tfrac{\text{miss}}{2}\right) - 100\,\bar t_{\text{compute}}
$$

**Warehouse.** One to eight differential-drive robots must pick up goals and deliver them to collection points
within 60 s, using only limited, occluded 2D LiDAR and no communication at run time. A collision costs 500 points.

## Approach

### Docking: plan with SCvx, track with LQR, replan on disturbances

Straight-line seeds collided with asteroids in *space-time* and never converged. A small reachability DP over
(arc length, speed, time) gives seeds that are already collision-free in time, and SCvx then converges in fewer than
20 iterations.

<Figure src={scvx} alt="SCvx convergence: cost and constraint violation over iterations" caption="SCvx convergence on a public case." basis="simulation" />

### Warehouse: plan everything centrally, verify it exactly, then execute

The simulator is deterministic, so a central planner can compute and **check the full timed trajectory of every
robot** before anything moves. Collisions and deadlocks are then ruled out by construction.

1. **Geometry.** Inflated obstacles, a visibility graph, and all-pairs shortest paths. Pickups only graze each goal disk
   via points just inside it.
2. **Allocation.** An exact subset DP prices every goal set per robot. Goals are split among robots exactly for ≤ 12
   goals and by local search beyond that.
3. **Coordination.** Prioritized space-time planning with alternative legs and detours, a search over priority orders,
   and repair of the critical robot (LNS).
4. **Execution.** Each agent replays its plan with an arc-fitting tracker, under 2 ms per tick.

- **Docking:** mean public score **+28.5 over the TA reference** (875.9). All 80 random scenarios succeed, including
  32 with disturbance bursts up to twice the public strength.
- **Warehouse:** every goal delivered with **zero collisions** on all public configs and in ~170 randomized and
  edge-case runs. In 7 over-full cases I proved a lower bound on delivery time above 60 s, and the planner switches
  to an orienteering objective that maximizes goals delivered.

## What I learned

- **Read the score as a specification.** Turning the grader into design drivers (one second of makespan = 10 points;
  driving backwards is free) decided most of the architecture before I wrote any code.
- **Seeds matter more than solvers.** Non-convex trajectory optimization succeeds or fails on its initial guess.
- **When a system is deterministic, verify instead of reacting.** Exact replay of the plan turned a hard online
  multi-robot problem into an offline search with a correctness check.
