## Problem

A stethoscope gives one listen at one moment. A **wearable** one could record heart and lung sounds continuously,
but on the body the heartbeat competes with breathing, muscle and contact noise, movement and room sound. The
signal-processing problem was to make the heartbeat clean and detectable in real time on a small device.

## Approach

- **Filter design first.** Heart sounds sit low in frequency, and much of the noise sits above them. I designed and
  compared FIR and IIR band-pass filters for attenuation, phase behaviour and latency. The chosen design improved SNR
  by **12 dB** within a **20 ms** processing budget.
- **Separate what filtering can't.** Lung sounds and some body noise overlap the heart band. NMF on the STFT
  magnitude learns spectral templates and activations, so the heart component can be kept and the others dropped.
  Wavelet transforms gave a second time–frequency view of the same recordings.
- **Detect beats.** Peak detection on the cleaned signal reached **96 % sensitivity**.

## What I learned

- **Start from the spectrum.** Measuring where the signal and the noise actually sit decided the filter order and
  band edges.
- **Latency is a design constraint, not an afterthought.** A zero-phase filter is ideal offline but not real-time. The
  deployed path has to trade phase distortion against delay.
- **Unsupervised methods need sanity checks.** NMF components carry no labels. Deciding which one is "heart" means
  checking it against what the heartbeat should look like in time and frequency.
